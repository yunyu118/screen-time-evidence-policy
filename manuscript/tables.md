**Table 3.** Priority research questions, matched study designs, data sources, and feasibility.

| Priority | Question | Design | Data source | Outcomes | Feasibility | Cost | Time to result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Do school phone restrictions change adolescent mental health, and does the effect depend on restriction intensity? | Staggered-adoption difference-in-differences across US states, with event-study specification and intensity coding | Youth Risk Behavior Surveillance System; state administrative and disciplinary records; existing statute effective dates | Depressive symptoms, suicidal ideation, suicide attempt, absenteeism | High. Data already collected. Principal constraint is time: the counterfactual disappears as adoption saturates. | Low | 12-18 months |
| 2 | Does restriction improve mental health only when the vacated time is filled, ie, is the mechanism displacement? | Three-arm cluster-randomized trial: restriction alone, restriction plus funded offline activity, usual practice | Primary collection; schools or municipalities as clusters | Validated wellbeing and depressive symptom scales, self-harm, device use, physical activity, sleep | Moderate. | 3 | Does a national minimum-age law change mental health, as distinct from account ownership? | Extend the existing Australian regression discontinuity to mental health endpoints, with a synthetic control from comparable non-adopting countries | Australian cohort already assembled; national self-harm presentation and mortality data | Depressive symptoms, self-harm presentations, suicide | High for symptoms, moderate for self-harm, low for suicide within one jurisdiction. | Low to moderate | 12-24 months |
| 4 | Can any of these policies detect an effect on suicide or self-harm at plausible effect sizes? | Coordinated multi-jurisdiction pooled analysis with prespecified harmonized outcome ascertainment; power analysis published in advance | National mortality and emergency presentation registries across adopting and non-adopting jurisdictions | Suicide death, self-harm presentation | Moderate.
